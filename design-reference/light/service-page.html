Section 138 • Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

CHEQUE BOUNCE MATTERS IN DELHI

Advocate Shubham Singh acts in criminal complaints and appellate proceedings arising under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 across the District Courts of Delhi and the High Court of Delhi. Representation extends to statutory demand notice issuance, complainant prosecution, defense trial advocacy, interim compensation applications under Section 143A, compounding, and appellate quashing under Section 482 Cr.P.C.

01 — Scope of Invocation

When This Applies

Proceedings under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act are triggered when a cheque presented for the discharge of a legally enforceable debt or liability is returned dishonoured by the bank. In commercial, corporate, and individual disputes across Delhi, the legal framework strictly regulates both criminal liability and structured opportunities for compounding.

For Complainants (Payees) RECOVERY / PROSECUTION

  • • Cheque dishonour with remarks "Funds Insufficient", "Account Closed", or "Payment Stopped".
  • • Drafting and dispatch of mandatory Legal Demand Notice within 30 days of receiving the memo.
  • • Filing of pre-summoning complaint before Metropolitan Magistrate upon expiry of the 15-day notice period.
  • • Seeking interim compensation up to 20% under Section 143A of the NI Act.

For Accused (Drawers) TRIAL DEFENSE & QUASHING

  • • Receipt of statutory demand notice or judicial summons from Delhi Metropolitan Magistrate Courts.
  • • Defense against misuse of blank security cheques given for non-existent legal debts.
  • • Rebuttal of statutory presumptions under Sections 118 and 139 via strict cross-examination.
  • • Petitions before High Court of Delhi under Section 482 Cr.P.C. for quashing malicious or time-barred complaints.
02 — Statutory Roadmap

How the Process Generally Works

Section 138 proceedings follow an exacting chronological statutory chain. Any deviation from statutory limitation periods can affect maintainability.

01

Bank Return Memo & Cause of Action

Validity: Within 3 Months

The cheque must be presented to the bank within its validity period (usually 3 months from issuance). When dishonoured, the bank issues a Return Memo specifying reasons such as "insufficiency of funds", "exceeds arrangement", or "account blocked". The date of memo receipt starts the statutory clock.

02

Statutory Demand Notice

Within 30 Days of Memo

A formal legal notice must be issued via registered post or speed post demanding payment of the cheque amount within 15 days of notice receipt. Precision in drafting is essential: the amount demanded must clearly correlate with the dishonoured cheque, without conflating unliquidated claims.

03

Filing of Criminal Complaint

Within 30 Days of Default

If the drawer fails to make payment within 15 days of receiving the legal notice, the cause of action arises on the 16th day. The complainant must institute a complaint under Section 138/142 before the competent Metropolitan Magistrate within 30 days thereafter, accompanied by affidavit and original documents.

04

Pre-Summoning & Issuance of Process

Judicial Scrutiny

The court examines the complainant's pre-summoning evidence by way of affidavit under Section 145 NI Act. Upon satisfaction of prima facie ingredients, summons or bailable warrants are issued to the accused to secure appearance and take plea under Section 251 Cr.P.C.

05

Trial, Section 143A & Final Judgment

Evidence & Cross-Examination

The summary or summons trial proceeds through cross-examination of complainant witnesses, examination of the accused under Section 313 Cr.P.C., and defense evidence. Courts may order interim compensation under Section 143A. Proceedings conclude either in conviction, acquittal, or compoundable settlement through mediation.

04 — Statutory Queries

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if the 30-day notice period is missed after cheque dishonour? + −
Under Section 138 proviso (b), issuing the statutory demand notice within 30 days of receiving the bank memo is a mandatory condition precedent. If missed, the complainant generally cannot institute a Section 138 complaint on that specific dishonour. However, if the cheque remains within its original 3-month validity period, it may in certain circumstances be re-presented to the bank to generate a fresh return memo and a new cause of action.
Can a cheque bounce matter be settled or compounded outside court? + −
Yes. Under Section 147 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, all offences under Section 138 are expressly compoundable. Parties may enter into a formal settlement agreement at any stage of proceedings — during pre-summoning, mediation cell proceedings, Lok Adalat, regular trial, or even in appeal before the High Court. Upon compounding and payment of agreed terms, the criminal proceedings stand terminated.
What is the significance of Section 143A (Interim Compensation)? + −
Inserted via the 2018 amendment, Section 143A empowers the trial court to order the drawer to deposit interim compensation up to 20% of the cheque amount in summary or summons trials where the accused pleads not guilty. As settled by the Supreme Court in Rakesh Ranjan Shrivastava v. State of Jharkhand (2024), this provision is discretionary, requiring the trial court to record reasons considering prima facie merits.
Is the director of a company automatically liable for a dishonoured corporate cheque? + −
No. Under Section 141 of the NI Act (Vicarious Liability), a director can only be arrayed as an accused if the complaint contains specific, distinct averments that such director was in charge of, and responsible to the company for the conduct of its business at the time the offence was committed. Independent, non-executive directors who are not signatories often seek quashing under Section 482 Cr.P.C. when vicarious liability lacks specific factual foundation.
