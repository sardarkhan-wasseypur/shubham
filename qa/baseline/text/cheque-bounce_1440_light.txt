The statutory framework provides a quasi-criminal remedy for the payee while maintaining strict statutory timelines for notices and complaints. Statutory Notice & Limitation Advisory Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, statutory time frames apply: a legal demand notice must generally be issued within 30 days of receiving the bank memo, providing 15 days for the drawer to pay. If payment is not made within 15 days, a criminal complaint must be filed before the competent Metropolitan Magistrate within 30 days of the cause of action arising. Consultation on Cheque Bounce Matters All Practice Areas [ 01 ] APPLICABILITY Who This Practice Helps • Payees, individuals, and business entities holding dishonoured cheques seeking statutory recovery. • Drawers and defendants served with legal demand notices or court summons under Section 138. • Commercial enterprises seeking compoundable settlement, mediation, or trial defence. [ 02 ] CIRCUMSTANCES When This Applies • A cheque presented within its validity period is returned unpaid due to insufficient funds or exceeding arrangements. • The drawer issues stop-payment instructions or closes the account before the cheque is encashed. • The underlying cheque was issued in discharge of a legally enforceable debt or liability. [ 03 ] PROCEDURAL STAGES How the Process Generally Works Standard procedural trajectory observed under Indian statutory timelines. Specific steps may vary by court and factual merits. STAGE 0 1 Bank Return Memo Receipt The cheque is returned unpaid by the drawee bank with an official return memo specifying the reason for dishonour. STAGE 0 2 Statutory Demand Notice (30 Days) A formal written notice of demand is dispatched to the drawer within 30 days of receiving the bank memo. STAGE 0 3 Fifteen-Day Settlement Window The drawer is afforded 15 clear days from receipt of the notice to remit the demanded cheque amount. STAGE 0 4 Complaint Filing (Within 30 Days) If payment is not made within the 15-day period, a formal criminal complaint must be instituted before the competent Metropolitan Magistrate within 30 days. STAGE 0 5 Cognizance, Summoning & Trial The court examines the complainant under Section 200 CrPC/BNSS, issues process/summons, frames notice of accusation, and conducts summary/regular trial or refers to Lok Adalat/mediation. For case-specific queries, consult the advocate. What is the mandatory timeline to issue a legal notice under Section 138? The legal demand notice must be dispatched within 30 days of receiving the bank dishonour memo. The drawer must then be granted 15 days to make payment. Can a Section 138 cheque bounce matter be settled through mediation? Yes. Offences under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act are compoundable. Courts encourage amicable resolution, Lok Adalat referral, or mutual settlement between the parties at any stage of proceedings. What occurs if the complainant misses the 30-day filing deadline? Under Section 142(1)(b) of the Act, the court may condone delay in filing the complaint if the complainant satisfies the Magistrate that there was sufficient cause for not filing within the prescribed period. Is interim compensation applicable during trial? Section 143A of the NI Act empowers the trial court to direct the drawer to pay interim compensation not exceeding 20% of the cheque amount during summary or regular trial. Can directors or partners be prosecuted for company cheques? Under Section 141 of the Act, if the person committing the offence is a company or partnership, individuals who were in charge of and responsible for the conduct of business at the time of the offence may be vicariously prosecuted, subject to specific averments in the complaint.
