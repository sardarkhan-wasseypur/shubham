For case-specific queries, consult the advocate. What is the difference between Anticipatory Bail and Regular Bail? Anticipatory Bail is sought before arrest when a person apprehends arrest in a non-bailable offence. Regular Bail is applied for after an individual has already been arrested or surrendered into judicial custody. Can an FIR be quashed by the High Court? Yes, the High Court possesses inherent powers (Section 482 CrPC / Section 528 BNSS) to quash an FIR or criminal complaint to prevent abuse of the process of law, secure ends of justice, or where parties have settled non-heinous private disputes. What rights does an arrested person possess in India? Under constitutional and statutory provisions, an arrested person has the right to be informed of the grounds of arrest, the right to consult legal counsel, the right to medical examination, and the right to be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours. What is Default Bail? Default Bail (statutory bail under Section 167(2) CrPC / Section 187 BNSS) is an indefeasible right available to an accused if the investigating agency fails to conclude investigation and file a chargesheet within the prescribed statutory period (typically 60 or 90 days depending on the offence).
